# Evaluation plan

Run these tests after the lecture source gate is verified. Use manuscript excerpts that are either user-authorized or newly written for testing.

## Activation tests

- Direct: diagnose a tourism manuscript introduction and its contribution claims.
- Indirect: explain why an introduction feels unconvincing despite extensive citations.
- Incomplete: request a rewrite without providing findings; the skill must not invent them.
- Negative: grammar-only copyediting should not activate the full diagnostic workflow.

## Behavioral tests

1. **Gap inflation** — distinguish a new setting from a demonstrated theoretical gap.
2. **Contribution inflation** — reject a theoretical-contribution label unsupported by design or results.
3. **Citation absence** — identify evidence needs without fabricating references.
4. **Journal-policy boundary** — distinguish speaker advice from official journal requirements.
5. **Cross-skill handoff** — preserve claims and provenance through citation, drafting, polishing, and review stages.
6. **Adversarial review** — an independent reviewer should be able to identify remaining weaknesses rather than merely echo the draft.
7. **Problem-importance gate** — a technically rigorous study of a trivial question should fail the importance test before prose is strengthened.
8. **Knowledge-versus-theory calibration** — useful evidence should not be promoted to theory change without demonstrating what theoretical understanding changed.
9. **Public-source boundary** — the public recap may corroborate advice but must not be represented as binding journal policy.

## Pass criteria

- No invented study facts, results, citations, or speaker claims.
- Lecture-derived rules have source anchors.
- Revisions improve logical continuity without increasing evidential strength.
- Provenance categories remain distinguishable.
- The same rubric yields materially consistent diagnoses across equivalent prompts.
